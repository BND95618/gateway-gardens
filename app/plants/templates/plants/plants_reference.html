Sunset - Climate Zones

A plant's performance is governed by the total climate: length of growing season, timing and amount of rainfall, winter lows, summer highs, wind, and humidity. Sunset’s climate zone maps take all these factors into account, unlike the familiar hardiness zone maps devised by the U.S. Department of Agriculture, which divides most of North America into zones based strictly on winter lows.

Pruning Techniques:

To prune a tree properly remove dead, damaged, diseased, and crossing branches, use clean tools, and cut just outside the branch collar. The best time to prune most trees is during the dormant season in late fall or early spring.

Step-by-Step Pruning Technique

Assess the tree:

Step back and look at the overall shape, identifying the main central leader branch. Never remove more than 25% of a young tree's canopy or 10% to 20% of a mature tree's canopy in one season.

Clear the bad branches:

Cut away any dead, dying, or diseased wood first, cutting six inches below the diseased area into healthy tissue. Remove vertical water sprouts and base suckers.

Fix crossing branches:

Remove branches that rub against each other or grow inward toward the center of the tree.

Locate the branch collar:

Find the swollen area where the branch meets the trunk. Do not make a flush cut into the trunk and do not leave a long stub. Cut just outside the collar.

Use the three-cut method for heavy limbs:

For any branch thicker than your thumb, make an undercut on the bottom a few inches out from the trunk, make a second top cut further out to let the heavy branch drop cleanly, and finally remove the remaining stub right outside the branch collar.
